Why so many MDM programs disappoint

Gartner estimates that three in four MDM programs miss their business objectives. In our experience the hub itself is rarely the problem. The trouble starts when a project is scoped around data, with no decision or process it is meant to improve, and the golden records end up sitting in a hub nobody downstream uses.

So we start every engagement from a business outcome: a sales team that sees one customer instead of three, a claims process that stops paying duplicate providers, a supplier list procurement can trust. We scope the smallest piece of MDM that delivers it, build that, and then expand.

Working that way keeps the inputs small and known, which is why we can usually give you a fixed price and a timeline before any open-ended discovery.

Common questions

How can you fix a price for MDM?

We scope around one or two specific use cases, so the project comes down to a short list of known inputs: entities, sources, consuming systems, hierarchies and volumes. If yours needs discovery first, or has a technical risk unique to your environment, we'll say so and shape the work around it.

Isn't MDM slow and expensive?

It used to be, mostly because of on-prem servers and multi-year builds. Informatica MDM SaaS takes most of that away. The quickest way to find out what yours would take is the questionnaire.

We run Informatica MDM on-prem. Can you move it without breaking anything?

Yes. We plan for your existing golden records and prove parity before cutover, so what the hub delivers today keeps working.

We use a different MDM. Can you replace it?

Yes. Replacements have more moving parts, so we scope them on a call. A national health insurer we worked with left its previous MDM ahead of a hard renewal deadline.

What happens after I fill in the questionnaire?

Someone on our team reads it. Within 72 hours you get a PDF with a cost range, a timeline and a complexity rating. If it looks right, we book a 45-minute call; if not, that's the end of it.
